# PRODUCT: FeatherLM blind A/B reading test

## Register
product (a single-purpose tool: read two texts, pick one, repeat 20 times)

## Users and context
- Hackathon attendees at the venue, handed one phone or opening a link, reading standing up, often one-handed, in bright indoor light, with 5 to 8 minutes of patience.
- Readers from a Telegram channel who open the link on their own phone, mostly signed out of claude.ai, so their picks leave as a vote code they paste back into the chat.
- The organisers (Ruslan, Ali), who share the link, hand the phone around and read the tally at the end.

## Purpose
Collect honest, blind human judgements of which of two rewrites of the same AI draft reads more like a skilled person wrote it. The page must never reveal which system wrote which side, must not bias the reader (side order shuffled, equal visual weight, no length or style hints), and must never lose a pick.

## Brand personality
Calm editorial. A quiet, bookish reading surface where the copy is the star. Minimal chrome, generous reading type, confident but unhurried. The reader should feel like a careful editor, not a survey respondent.

## Anti-references
- Generic SaaS survey forms (Typeform / Google Forms): rounded card stacks, bright blue pill buttons, progress-bar-and-emoji cheerfulness.

## Accessibility
WCAG AA contrast in light and dark, full keyboard voting (1 / 2 / 0, Enter, Left arrow), visible focus, works one-handed on a 375 px phone, honours reduced motion.

## Strategic principles
1. Reading comes first: both rewrites get identical treatment and comfortable measure.
2. Zero bias: no system names, no colour coding of sides, no hints from layout.
3. No lost picks: every pick is saved to the shared store, or to the device plus a vote code when the store is out of reach.
4. Fast to finish: 20 pairs, one clear decision per pair, the next pair one tap away.
