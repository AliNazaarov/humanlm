# Judge Q&A: 12 likely questions, short answers

Numbers marked (audit) are from `hackathon/audit/AUDIT.md` as generated on 2026-11-02 (re-run `python3 hackathon/audit/audit.py` before the finals and update them). Numbers marked [RESULT] come from `hackathon/report/results.json`. Say nothing that is not in one of those two files.

## 1. Why not just prompt GPT or Claude?

We did, with the same system prompt and items. GPT-6.1 keeps facts but leaves the AI tells in: it passes the tells gate on 46% of items (audit, G3 column). Claude Opus 5.5 removes the tells but cuts: it deletes at least one draft sentence on 51% of items and drops or changes numbers (audit, section C and E). Neither gives you both at once. Our loop does, on a model that costs [RESULT: $/1k, O3] per thousand rewrites against [RESULT: $/1k, Claude strong] for Claude. And the fairness row shows the loop is the product: Claude plus our harness reaches [RESULT: F1H rate], at [RESULT: F1H $/1k].

## 2. Isn't the harness just gaming the metric it is scored on?

That was our first worry, so we audited it with signals the harness never sees (`hackathon/audit/AUDIT.md`, regenerated by one script):

- Gate fairness: the gates' regex checks over-penalise harmless edits (a spelled-out numeral, `Kenyan` -> `Kenya`). With those forgiven, the harness still leads the best Claude row by 11 points, 97% vs 85% (audit, section E). The gap shrinks from 21 to 11; it does not vanish.
- Dropped content: the harness deletes a draft sentence on 17% of items; Claude on about 50% (audit, section C). Lower is better, and the gates do not measure this.
- Closeness to the real human original on the 120 held-out pairs: the harness is closer than both Claude rows (+0.03 chrF, significant) and slightly further than GPT-6.1 (-0.02), because GPT edits least (audit, section A).
- Where we lose: on six tells outside the gates (hedges, tricolons, burstiness, lexical variety...), Claude is 3 to 4 points more in the human band than we are; GPT is a tie (audit, section B). We say that on the slide.
- Copy rate: 10% of harness outputs are near-copies of the draft (word-bigram F1 above 0.9) against 1-2% for the frontier rows. The base model without the loop is at 7%, so the loop adds about 3 points of conservatism. Disclosed, not hidden.

Verdict in the audit's own words: harness wins on gate fairness, content retention and artefacts; mixed on closeness and on hold-out tells; partly gaming on copy rate.

## 3. Where does the data come from, and is it licensed?

Human text only from Common Pile filtered subsets (openly licensed or public domain: news, cccc, Project Gutenberg, Foodista, Public Domain Review). We reverse-distil: an open model writes the AI-polished version of each human passage, and the training pair is draft -> human. No frontier-model text is ever a training target. The eval sets are never in training: `hackathon/data/make_split.py` drops any pair sharing 8+ consecutive words with an eval draft, and `hackathon/data/SPLIT.md` records 0 remaining overlaps. The 120 held-out pairs were carved by pid hash before the training set was built.

## 4. How do you measure "human"?

Not with a detector. `pipeline/metrics.py` scores 16 separate tells (stock phrases, em dashes, not-X-but-Y, emoji, exclamations, hedges, tricolons, burstiness, lexical variety...) and asks whether each sits inside the 10th-90th percentile band of 909 real human texts. The headline gate uses five of them; the audit checks six more the harness never sees. Humanness proper is the pairwise judge and the blind human test in `hackathon/report/` [RESULT: judge win rate, both orders, if run]. We treat the metrics as a screen, not as the truth.

## 7. Why GLM-5.3 Flash?

It is on Fireworks serverless, cheap ([RESULT: price from PRICES.md]), fast (p50 around a second for a single call, [RESULT]), and good enough that a verifier loop lifts it from 23% first-draft pass to 93% (audit, section C). We also tried Nemotron on serverless; latency ran to minutes per item and the runs did not finish, which is in the logs.

## 8. Business model?

An API priced per rewrite with a fact-safety guarantee, sold to founders, marketers and agencies first, then to agent builders who need a "publish-safe" step. Usage-based, with a free tier for single drafts. The guarantee is the product: the verifier report (what was kept, what was checked) ships with every response.

## 9. What is the moat against Grammarly or the humanizer tools?

Three things they do not have: licence-clean reverse-distillation data at scale (they would need the same pipeline and the same licence discipline), a verifier that is both the eval and the RL reward so the model gets better at the exact thing the customer buys, and the pick/edit loop: every time a user chooses between two rewrites we get preference data. Humanizers optimise for detector evasion; we refuse that market and never use a detector as a reward.

## 11. Failure cases?

From the run files: the harness still fails 18 items after revision (audit, section C), mostly G1 "attribution" flags (an extra "says" or "added") and reworded quotations. 10% of outputs are near-copies of the draft when the draft was already fine. On already-human drafts in the trick set the right answer is to change little, and the gates reward that, which is a bias worth naming. And the gates are regex: they cannot catch a paraphrase that changes meaning without touching a number or a name.
