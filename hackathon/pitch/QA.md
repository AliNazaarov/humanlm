## 1. Your results look too good. How?

Say where we lose first, then how the number is made. Final numbers, all from `results.json` unless marked.

1. **Where we lose: style.** Blind human A/B, O3c vs Claude strong: 209 votes from 45 readers (frozen 15:15 PT), Claude 89, ours 64, both fine 56, sign test p=0.052, not significant at 0.05; p=0.029 after the stated reading-time rule drops 11 too-fast ballots (`human_votes.json`). Judges are split and neither is significant on O3c: Kimi K3 style 33/46/51 (leans Claude, p=0.177), Qwen 3.8 Max style 44/27/37 (leans ours, p=0.057). Readers' choppy tells (fragment runs, slogans, hooks) show up in 37.7% of our outputs vs 10.0% of Claude's on the main split (`reader_tells.json`). We do not claim better prose.
2. **The gain is the harness, not a smarter model.** Draft, check every number, name, quote and attribution plus AI tells and length, fix only what failed, at most 3 rounds. Proof: Claude Opus 5.5 inside the same loop (F1H) scores 93.5% [89.7, 96.3] on all 247 items vs our 96.8% [93.7, 98.6], at $15.85 vs $0.81 per 1k (about 20x). Claude with a strong prompt alone: 71.3% [65.2, 76.8] at $9.11 (about 11x ours).
3. **It transfers.** Same harness on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (R3): 94.7% [91.2, 97.2]. Ensemble of seven harness variants (E1): 99.2% [97.1, 99.9], at $15.12 per 1k.
4. **Evidence outside our own tuning set:** 80 real GitHub READMEs never used to tune anything: 97.5% vs Claude strong 76.2%, paired +21 pp [+13, +31]. Held-out real pairs excluding any id that overlaps SFT data (96 items): 99.0% vs 71.9%. A do-nothing control (copy the draft) scores 31.6%, so the gates are not trivially passable.
5. **Why Claude scores lower on this metric:** it rewrites boldly, shortens and drops lines: G2 (nothing lost) 88% and G1 (nothing invented) 88% on all 247. With the gates' regex artefacts forgiven (audit, its own 250-item run), Claude strong is 85% [81, 89] and O3c 98% [95, 99]: the lead shrinks but holds.
6. **Disclosed:** many harness variants were tuned on the same main items; that is why the README and held-out-clean splits exist. An early best-of-3 variant (O3j) invented soft details the regex gates miss (Kimi facts 4/25), so we added a claim checker (O3k) instead of shipping it.

Closing line: "We didn't make an open model smarter than Claude. We made it accountable, and made accountability about 20x cheaper." (20 = F1H $15.85 / O3c $0.81 per 1k, `results.json: systems[].cost_per_1k_usd`; see FINAL_CHECK.md.)

## 2. Why not just prompt GPT or Claude?

We did, with the same system prompt and items. GPT-6.1 keeps facts but leaves the AI tells in: it passes the tells gate on 46% of items (audit, G3 column). Claude Opus 5.5 removes the tells but cuts: it deletes at least one draft sentence on 51% of items and drops or changes numbers (audit, section C and E). Neither gives you both at once. Our loop does, on a model that costs $0.28 (O3; our entry O3c: $0.81) per thousand rewrites against $9.11 for Claude Opus 5.5 strong. And the fairness row shows the loop is the product: Claude plus our harness reaches 94% fact-safe (HEADLINE.md, all 247 items), at $15.85 per 1k rewrites.

## 3. Isn't the harness just gaming the metric it is scored on?

That was our first worry, so we audited it with signals the harness never sees (`hackathon/audit/AUDIT.md`, regenerated by one script):

- Gate fairness: the gates' regex checks over-penalise harmless edits (a spelled-out numeral, `Kenyan` -> `Kenya`). With those forgiven, the harness still leads the best Claude row by 11 points, 97% vs 85% (audit, section E). The gap shrinks from 21 to 11; it does not vanish.
- Dropped content: the harness deletes a draft sentence on 17% of items; Claude on about 50% (audit, section C). Lower is better, and the gates do not measure this.
- Closeness to the real human original on the 120 held-out pairs: the harness is closer than both Claude rows (+0.03 chrF, significant) and slightly further than GPT-6.1 (-0.02), because GPT edits least (audit, section A).
- Where we lose: on six tells outside the gates (hedges, tricolons, burstiness, lexical variety...), Claude is 3 to 4 points more in the human band than we are; GPT is a tie (audit, section B). We say that on the slide.
- Copy rate: 10% of harness outputs are near-copies of the draft (word-bigram F1 above 0.9) against 1-2% for the frontier rows. The base model without the loop is at 7%, so the loop adds about 3 points of conservatism. Disclosed, not hidden.

Verdict in the audit's own words: harness wins on gate fairness, content retention and artefacts; mixed on closeness and on hold-out tells; partly gaming on copy rate.

## 4. Where does the data come from, and is it licensed?

Human text only from Common Pile filtered subsets (openly licensed or public domain: news, cccc, Project Gutenberg, Foodista, Public Domain Review). We reverse-distil: an open model writes the AI-polished version of each human passage, and the training pair is draft -> human. No frontier-model text is ever a training target. The eval sets are never in training: `hackathon/data/make_split.py` drops any pair sharing 8+ consecutive words with an eval draft, and `hackathon/data/SPLIT.md` records 0 remaining overlaps. The 120 held-out pairs were carved by pid hash before the training set was built.

## 5. How do you measure "human"?

Not with a detector. `pipeline/metrics.py` scores 16 separate tells (stock phrases, em dashes, not-X-but-Y, emoji, exclamations, hedges, tricolons, burstiness, lexical variety...) and asks whether each sits inside the 10th-90th percentile band of 909 real human texts. The headline gate uses five of them; the audit checks six more the harness never sees. Humanness proper is the pairwise judge and the blind human test in `hackathon/report/` (O3c vs Claude Opus 5.5 strong, both orders: Kimi K3 style 33 ours / 46 Claude, Qwen 3.8 Max style 44 / 27, neither significant; humans prefer Claude's prose, see human_votes.json). We treat the metrics as a screen, not as the truth.

## 6. Cost and latency?

From `results.json`: harness on GLM-5.3 Flash (O3c) $0.81 per 1k rewrites, p50 3.4 s; Claude Opus 5.5 strong $9.11 per 1k rewrites, p50 3.7 s; GPT-6.1 Terra $0.85 per 1k rewrites, p50 2.6 s. The harness makes up to four model calls per item (median loops used: 1; distribution 57/105/61/27 across 0/1/2/3 loops, audit section C), which is why it is not the cheapest row, only the cheapest row that passes.

## 7. What was built today?

Everything under `hackathon/`: the held-out split and leakage check, Fireworks runners, the gates and the harness, the Claude and GPT runs, scoring with paired bootstrap CIs, the report, the audit, the demo, this deck. Pre-existing and disclosed: the corpus pipeline, ~7.8k raw pairs, `metrics.py` with its tests, the 130-item eval set, the Claude persona baseline. No trained model was brought in.

## 8. Why GLM-5.3 Flash?

It is on Fireworks serverless, cheap (O3c costs $0.81 per 1k rewrites including up to four calls per item), fast (O3c p50 3.4 s end to end), and good enough that a verifier loop lifts it from 23% first-draft pass to 93% (audit, section C). We also tried Nemotron on serverless; latency ran to minutes per item and the runs did not finish, which is in the logs.

## 9. Business model?

An API priced per rewrite with a fact-safety guarantee, sold to founders, marketers and agencies first, then to agent builders who need a "publish-safe" step. Usage-based, with a free tier for single drafts. The guarantee is the product: the verifier report (what was kept, what was checked) ships with every response.

## 10. What is the moat against Grammarly or the humanizer tools?

Three things they do not have: licence-clean reverse-distillation data at scale (they would need the same pipeline and the same licence discipline), a verifier that is both the eval and the RL reward so the model gets better at the exact thing the customer buys, and the pick/edit loop: every time a user chooses between two rewrites we get preference data. Humanizers optimise for detector evasion; we refuse that market and never use a detector as a reward.

## 11. Where are the SFT and RL rows?

SFT A (5,000 pairs) trained on the Fireworks Serverless Training API and was promoted, but could not be served on Ali's account (serverless LoRA disabled, deployment needs a payment method). SFT B (modern web, 2,924 rows) and SFT C (gate-clean, 1,283 rows) trained on Ruslan's account and were evaluated through a brief live-merge deployment (deleted after): alone, without the harness, they score 32.8% and 37.7% fact-safe vs 26.7% for the base model, so training helps but the harness does most of the work. A local Mac DPO run (Qwen3-4B, 70 steps) is a partial pipeline demo (18 rows). Details: hackathon/train/TRAIN_LOG.md.

## 12. Failure cases?

From the run files: the harness still fails 18 items after revision (audit, section C), mostly G1 "attribution" flags (an extra "says" or "added") and reworded quotations. 10% of outputs are near-copies of the draft when the draft was already fine. On already-human drafts in the trick set the right answer is to change little, and the gates reward that, which is a bias worth naming. And the gates are regex: they cannot catch a paraphrase that changes meaning without touching a number or a name.
