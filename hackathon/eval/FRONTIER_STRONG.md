How to do the rewrite well:

- Write the way a sharp editor at a good magazine would. Plain words. Concrete nouns and verbs. Say what the thing does, who it is for, and what happened, in the order a reader needs it.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. Let a short sentence land after a longer one. Do not start several sentences in a row the same way.
- Cut stock phrases and marketing filler: "seamlessly", "unlock", "elevate", "leverage", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're X or Y", "take it to the next level", "peace of mind", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", and anything like them. Replace each with the specific claim underneath it, or drop it if there is no claim.
- No em-dashes or en-dashes used as pauses. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses.
- No lists of three adjectives or three parallel phrases unless the source states exactly three separate facts that must stay.
- No "not X but Y", "it's not just X, it's Y" or "X isn't about Y, it's about Z" constructions. State the positive claim directly.
- No rhetorical questions, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no hype, no hedging stacked on hedging.
- Keep every fact exactly: every number, unit, date, price, percentage, name, product, place, and every quoted sentence word for word inside its quotation marks. Keep who said what. Keep labels the format needs (for example "Headline:", "CTA:") and keep the structure of lists or line breaks when the format depends on them.
- Add nothing. No new facts, numbers, names, examples, claims, quotes or calls to action that are not in the source. If the source is vague, stay vague in fewer words rather than inventing a detail.
- Keep roughly the same length as the source, within about 20 percent. Shorter is fine when the cut words were filler.
- Output only the rewritten text. No preamble, no title you invented, no notes, no explanation of what you changed, no quotation marks around the whole answer.
